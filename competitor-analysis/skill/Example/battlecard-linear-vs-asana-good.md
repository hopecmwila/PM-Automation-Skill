# Battlecard: Asana
_Last updated: 2026-09-22_
_Saved as: linear-competitor-analysis-2026-09-22.md_

## Quick Overview
- What they do: a general-purpose work-management platform for organizing, tracking, and managing
  team work, from daily tasks to strategic goals `[Confirmed]` (Source: g2.com/compare/asana-vs-linear, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Target customer: cross-functional teams and marketing operations, across company sizes and
  industries `[Confirmed]` (Source: temperstack.com/versus/asana-vs-linear, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Pricing model: free "Personal" plan for up to 10 users; Starter at $10.99/user/mo billed
  annually ($13.49 billed monthly); Advanced at $24.99/user/mo billed annually ($30.49 monthly);
  Enterprise on custom pricing `[Confirmed]` (Source: gend.co/blog/asana-pricing, accessed
  2026-09-22; page dated 28 Jan 2026).
- Recent developments: **Not found** - no confirmed 2026 pricing or plan-structure change beyond
  the tiers above turned up in this pass. Worth a fresh check before using this in an active deal,
  since both products' pricing pages have moved more than once in the last year.

## Their Pitch
- Self-description: positions itself as the platform that connects daily tasks to company-wide
  strategy and goals `[Confirmed]` (Source: getguru.com/reference/linear-vs-asana, accessed 2026-09-22).
- Top claimed differentiators: task dependencies, Timeline/Gantt view, Workflow Builder
  automation, unlimited portfolios, Goals/OKR tracking, native time tracking (Advanced tier),
  Salesforce/Tableau/Power BI integrations `[Confirmed]` (Source: tinycommand.com/blogs/asana-pricing-2026, accessed 2026-09-22).

## Strengths (be honest)
- Meaningfully broader feature surface for large, cross-functional programs - dependencies,
  unlimited portfolios, Goals, and BI/CRM integrations that Linear doesn't offer at any tier
  `[Confirmed]` (Source: tinycommand.com/blogs/asana-pricing-2026, accessed 2026-09-22).
- G2's review-aggregate summary describes Asana's onboarding as straightforward, crediting an
  intuitive setup wizard `[Estimated]` (Source: g2.com/compare/asana-vs-linear, accessed
  2026-09-22 - AI-aggregated review sentiment, not a direct quote).
- Large, mature review base: 13,000+ G2 reviews at 4.4/5, versus Linear's 66-69 reviews at
  4.5/5 on the same page `[Confirmed]` (Source: g2.com/compare/asana-vs-linear, accessed
  2026-09-22) - a proof-of-scale argument worth taking seriously, even where Linear's per-review
  score is comparable.

## Weaknesses
- The free "Personal" plan caps at 10 users and offers only list/board/calendar views - no
  Timeline, automation, or unlimited dashboards, which are Starter-tier features `[Confirmed]`
  (Source: tinycommand.com/blogs/asana-pricing-2026, accessed 2026-09-22).
- No purpose-built issue-tracking or sprint/cycle workflow for software teams - Asana's model is
  general task management, not engineering-specific `[Inferred]` (based on the absence of
  issue-tracking language across Asana's own plan-feature descriptions; Source: gend.co/blog/asana-pricing, accessed 2026-09-22).
- The jump from Starter to Advanced more than doubles the per-seat price ($10.99 to $24.99/user/mo
  annual) to unlock workload management, approvals, and native time tracking `[Confirmed]`
  (Source: tinycommand.com/blogs/asana-pricing-2026, accessed 2026-09-22).
- One review-aggregation source shows a notably lower Trustpilot score (1.6/5, 288 reviews) than
  its G2/Capterra scores (4.4-4.5/5, 11,000+ reviews each), which that source ties to billing
  complaints `[Estimated]` (Source: rfp.wiki/vendors/asana/linear, accessed 2026-09-22) - flagged
  as a real divergence worth probing, not a settled fact about billing practices.

## Our Differentiators (Linear)
- Purpose-built issue tracking with cycles, sprints, and triage workflows, versus Asana's
  general-purpose task model `[Confirmed - subject]` (Source: linear.app/pricing, accessed
  2026-09-22).
- Entry paid tier undercuts Asana's on price: $10/user/mo (annual) vs. Asana's $10.99/user/mo
  (annual), with unlimited issues and file uploads included at that tier `[Confirmed]` (Source:
  linear.app/pricing, accessed 2026-09-22).
- G2's review-aggregate summary singles out Linear's speed and keyboard-first design as
  distinctive enough to have influenced other tools' UX `[Estimated]` (Source:
  g2.com/compare/asana-vs-linear, accessed 2026-09-22 - aggregated sentiment, not a direct quote).

## Objection Handling
| If the prospect says... | Respond with... |
|---|---|
| "Asana has more features" | True for cross-functional/marketing use. For a software team's core issue/sprint workflow specifically, Linear's narrower model needs less configuration to reach the same outcome - worth a side-by-side trial on their actual backlog. |
| "Asana's free plan works fine for us" | Check team size first: Asana's free Personal plan caps at 10 users with no Timeline/automation (Source: tinycommand.com, accessed 2026-09-22). Linear's free plan has no user cap; its limit is 250 issues and 2 teams instead (Source: linear.app/pricing, accessed 2026-09-22) - which constraint bites first depends on the team's shape. |

## Landmines to Set
- Ask whether the team needs native sprint/cycle planning - a real gap in Asana's model relative
  to Linear's, per the sources above.
- Ask what runs out first for them: task volume or headcount. Asana's free-tier constraint is
  users; Linear's is issues - this changes which tool a given team hits a wall with sooner.

## Win/Loss Themes
- Win: software/product teams that want an opinionated, fast issue tracker and don't need
  cross-functional portfolio or Goals/OKR reporting.
- Loss risk: cross-functional programs spanning marketing, ops, and engineering together, where
  Asana's broader feature set and non-engineer-friendly UI outweigh Linear's speed advantage -
  worth naming honestly rather than contesting every deal.
